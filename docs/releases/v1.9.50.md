they're broken. Don't ask me permission." Cut at `1a7a1899` (#1319: red `39de8c92`, green `0edc7f14`, red `05a0edc9`, green

## Before you upgrade

No migration, no stored format change. New public API: `InstantProcessSuspensions.recordResume(afterSuspensionOf:)`,
`InstantMaintenanceActivity` (`.live`, the default, and `.unbounded`) with `InstantMaintenanceActivityToken`, and
`InstantRuntimeConfiguration.maintenanceActivity`. An app that already notices its own suspensions (a watchdog thread
that ticks) should hand each one to `recordResume`; without it the gate still leaves out the time the device slept.

## Release focus

**A live-query prune batch reads only what it can drop** (#1319). On a device a prune batch held the operation gate
1.0-3.5 s, and on a copy of that device's store (46 stored results, 973 unloaded results of 167 MB, 514,406 facts) the
prune took 30 holds on a Mac, 28 over 250 ms, longest 660 ms. Each batch materialized 31,000-57,000 facts for a few
hundred entities: the whole-entity rule (#259) read every fact of each entity it might drop, a recording keeps its
has-many links on itself (2,888 attachment links on one), and every entity an earlier batch kept was read again in each
later batch. Now the hot store answers a batch with which released facts it holds and how many facts it holds of each
entity, and materializes only the entities all of whose held facts were released; a kept entity is checked again only
in a batch that releases another of its facts; a released chunk names its facts from its ownership rows. The decisions
are the rule's, from the same facts.

Measured on that copy, Mac, Debug, every stored result closed: 11.98 s and holds up to 660 ms before; 1.43-1.50 s and
every hold without a WAL checkpoint 5-55 ms after, with the same end state (46 results unloaded, 20,132 facts dropped).
With the newest 20 results open: 24 holds, 1.24 s, every hold without a checkpoint at most 55 ms.

**The prune never runs across a suspension** (#1319). The device's batch took the gate 3 s after the app left the
front; iOS suspended the app in the middle of it, and the batch held the gate until the app ran again 37 minutes later.
The prune now works inside `InstantMaintenanceActivity.live`, the system's expiring activity on iOS, iPadOS, watchOS,
tvOS and visionOS, and stops before its next batch when the system is about to suspend the process or refuses it time;
the batches left wait for the next prune. A batch whose activity expires while it waits for its gates (the server-apply
gate, then the operation gate) gives both back unworked: the device's prune also held the server-apply gate 86 minutes,
20:26:51 to 21:53:00, across a 2,637 s suspension that began as its background time ran out.

**Every gate counts the time the process ran** (#1319): the operation, server-apply and connection gates alike. The
operation gate reported that batch holding it 2,236,773 ms
and an outbox release waiting 391,652 ms: iOS had suspended the app about 1,564 s of it and the device slept about
681 s, and the gate timed on the wall clock. Waits, holds, aging and the 5 s stall threshold now run on
`CLOCK_UPTIME_RAW` minus the suspensions the app records, and `serial-gate.waited` and `serial-gate.stalled` add
`waitSuspendedMilliseconds`, `previousHolderSuspendedMilliseconds`, `holderSuspendedMilliseconds` and
`longestWaitSuspendedMilliseconds` beside the fields they had, whose names are unchanged.
