## Release focus

A relaunch no longer waits out the delivery claims that an ended launch left behind (#990, #1000).

A delivery claim is a durable lease on the ordered outbox head: until the claim is answered, released, or its
acknowledgement deadline passes, no other claimant delivers. The deadline is six seconds times the claim's in-flight
ordinal. Each runtime had a fresh random claimant id, a claim pass admitted nothing while another claimant held a live
claim, it reclaimed only claims whose deadline had passed, and it slept until the earliest deadline of anyone. A runtime
releases its own claims when its socket ends; a process that ends without that release (killed, crashed, replaced by an
update) cannot. So the next launch reclaimed one claim per six-second wake and offered nothing until the last one
passed: 18 such claims held a relaunch's delivery for 97 s on a device, with an acknowledgement timeout logged every
6 s and nothing acknowledged.

Upstream `Reactor.js` keeps no durable claims: on `init-ok` of a new socket, `_flushPendingMessages` sends every pending
mutation again, in order (`client/packages/core/src/Reactor.js`). A claim whose process has ended can never be
answered, so this release releases it at once instead of waiting out its deadline.

## What changed

1. **Claimant ids name the process and the launch.** `InstantOutboxClaimantProcess` builds
   `process-<pid>-<launch>/<runtime>`; the explicit flush lane appends its token as before.
2. **The claim releases an ended claimant's claims first.** Inside the claim transaction, before expired claims are
   reclaimed, the store releases every claim whose claimant has ended, fenced by that exact claimant id. A claimant has
   ended when it names this process's identifier with another launch (one identifier belongs to one process at a
   time), or when no process with its identifier exists (`kill(pid, 0)` answers `ESRCH`; any other answer, `EPERM`
   from a sandbox included, means a process exists). Released rows keep `delivery_started`, so the next offer is still
   treated as a possible re-send. The release is logged as `outbox.claims.ended-claimant-released` with
   `waitAvoidedMilliseconds`, not as acknowledgement timeouts.
3. **Ids written before 1.9.12 name no process.** On iOS, tvOS, watchOS and visionOS they count as ended: an app and its
   extensions update together, and the update ends every process of the old build. On macOS and Mac Catalyst they keep
   their lease, because processes of different builds can share one store there.
4. **What stays the same.** A claim of a process that still runs (an app extension that shares the store) keeps its
   lease, and so does a claim whose process identifier the system gave to another process. Deadlines, the in-flight
   window, ordering and the outbox rows are unchanged; no schema, migration or public API changes.

## Not changed here

- The refusal of a re-send after a relaunch is still labelled a first offer and fails terminally, because which writes
  an earlier connection offered is kept in memory only; the rows' durable `delivery_started` bit could drive it.
- The explicit flush lane still waits out an ended claimant's claims.
