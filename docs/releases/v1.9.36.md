# Instant Swift Data 1.9.36

Status: published under the maintainer's standing authorization for fixes, typed on 2026-10-15: "Fix things when
they're broken. Don't ask me permission." and the instruction of 2026-10-23 to land every green step on main. The last
code commit is `a4ee2959`. It carries 1.9.35 (#1072).

## Release focus

**The delivery pump's claim searches only the writes it may claim, not every refused one** (#1108, #1114; red
`d59cf398`, green `a4ee2959`).

After every write and every acknowledgement the pump's claim pass reads the next writes to deliver and checks whether
another remains. Both reads filtered `delivery_claim_state = ? AND (delivery_state = ? OR legacy metadata)`, and the
`OR` left SQLite one plan: a search on the claim state alone and a sort. Every ready row was visited, refused writes
included, and each through its body's overflow pages, because the delivery columns sit after the body. On one phone's
store that was 663 refused writes; after 1.9.35 the claim was the largest holder of the operation gate while recording,
402-524 ms of every recording minute on the phone-shaped store, about 60% of the gate (#1072).

Each read is now its two branches. The rows ready and in need of delivery are searched by a new partial index,
`instant_outbox_delivery_candidate_idx` (migration `0029_outbox_delivery_candidate_index`, reasserted at bootstrap),
whose condition the statements repeat as literals; legacy rows are searched by `instant_outbox_delivery_metadata_idx`.
The index holds only claimable rows, so a refused write, a claim and an acknowledgement write no page of it: #1007
dropped a full index on the same columns because every row write paid for it while the `OR` kept every statement off
it.

## Measured

On #1072's real-size fixture (version 3: integer transaction ids), Mac mini M4, release, shared with other agents'
builds:

- **The reads themselves** (`OutboxClaimScaleTests`, deterministic): beside 400 refused writes the candidate read took
  3,218 SQLite steps and the continuation check 3,223, against 74 and 79 beside 7: about eight steps a refused row,
  each through its body. After the fix both cost the same beside 400 as beside 7.
- **A refused write at the phone's size** (663 refused writes already in the outbox): 107.3 million instructions and
  8.0 ms of the gate in the claim per write before, 68.1 million and 3.4 ms after; its instructions across a store four
  times larger went from 2.1x to 1.4x.
- **A recording's minutes** (`RealSizeRecordingReport`, 120 minutes, the consuming app's shape): the claim's
  operation-gate time per minute, median over the two hours, from 475 ms to 298 ms (minutes 1-10: 569 to 267 ms;
  51-60: 517 to 221 ms; 111-120: 435 to 343 ms). The second run's machine was busier (each simulated minute took 35%
  longer), which inflates every hold; the step counts above are the load-free measure. What the claim still holds is
  its own work on the writes it claims (their rows, bodies and receipts), about 2 ms a pass.

## What stays the same

No public API change. The pump claims the same writes in the same order: the two branches are the old filter's two
sides, and `UNION` keeps a row that matches both once. One migration adds the index; a store opened by an older build
keeps it and ignores it.
